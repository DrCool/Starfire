# The Broken Star

(Late-Game Quest: Acquiring a Personal Starship)

Owning a personal starship in the Starfire galaxy is not a matter of wealth alone. While advanced players may accumulate enough credits to commission a ship’s hull, drive systems, and control architecture, the final obstacle is power. Modern shipyards no longer have unrestricted access to Starfire cores. Existing supplies are allocated to military contracts, essential infrastructure, or Continuum-sanctioned transport fleets.

When a player attempts to purchase a private starship, the shipyard provides a blunt explanation:
“We can build it. We just can’t power it.”

Through rumors, offhand comments, or discreet conversations, the player learns of an alternative. Long ago, a deep-range vessel crashed on a remote, largely unregulated planet beyond standard shuttle routes. The ship was Starfire-powered, and its engine core was never officially recovered. If the core still exists—and if it can be extracted—it could serve as the power source for a private ship.

Reaching the planet is a challenge in itself. The player must find transportation outside normal routes: hiring a fringe pilot, securing passage on a mercenary vessel, or earning a favor that grants access to a one-way trip. The journey reinforces a simple truth of the frontier: nothing moves unless someone makes it worth their while.

The crash site is vast and silent. The wrecked ship lies partially buried, its hull torn open by atmospheric entry. Emergency bulkheads sealed sections of the vessel centuries ago, and intermittent power still flickers deep within. Local wildlife has claimed the wreck, drawn by residual heat and energy leakage from damaged Starfire systems. These creatures are not malicious—they are simply adapted to an environment shaped by old technology bleeding into the landscape.

Inside the ship, conditions deteriorate. Gravity fluctuates. Automated defenses activate sporadically. Internal drones continue to perform maintenance tasks that no longer make sense. The ship’s onboard AI is fragmented, operating under incomplete emergency directives and refusing commands it does not recognize as valid. It is not hostile, but it is unyielding.

At the heart of the wreck lies the Starfire engine. The core is damaged but intact, its containment shielding degraded. Extracting it is dangerous. A rushed removal risks destabilizing the wreck and damaging the core itself. A careful extraction requires time, skill, and surviving the ship’s interior long enough to do the job properly.

The player must choose how to proceed. A careful salvage yields a more stable, reliable core—one that will serve their future ship well. A reckless extraction succeeds more quickly but leaves the core compromised, introducing long-term risks and quirks into the vessel that will one day rely on it.

When the player returns to the shipyard with the recovered Starfire core, the technicians say little. They inspect the core, install additional limiters, and warn that such power always comes with consequences. The ship is completed—not as a pristine triumph of modern engineering, but as a vessel powered by inherited force, carrying the marks of an older age.

The player does not receive a perfect ship.
They receive freedom, responsibility, and a reminder that in the Starfire galaxy, progress often means learning how to live with what the past has left behind.
