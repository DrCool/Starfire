# Starfall Gate — The Gravity Plate, Echelon, and Ashdown

This document defines the canonical structure, physics, culture, and gameplay rules for **Echelon** (the upper city) and **Ashdown** (the lower city) on Starfall Gate. It is intended as a long-term reference for generating rooms, NPCs, quests, events, and mechanics.

---

## The Gravity Plate

At the core of Starfall Gate lies an ancient gravity plate separating Echelon and Ashdown.

- **Thickness:** Approximately **15 feet**
- **Material:** Unknown alien alloy, smooth, dark, and unmarked
- **Function:** Generates a perfect, uniform **1g gravity field** toward its surface on *both* sides
- **Power Source:** Unknown; no visible feeds, conduits, or maintenance interfaces
- **Reliability:** Has never failed, fluctuated, or degraded

Despite its relatively thin profile, the plate supports the full structural load of megacities built on both faces while maintaining stable gravity. Modern engineering cannot replicate or fully explain its behavior.

Most scholars believe the gravity plate predates Starfall Gate itself and was **discovered**, not built, by modern civilizations. Echelon was constructed on top of this artifact rather than creating it.

> **Common belief:**  
> Echelon governs Starfall Gate — but it does not control what it stands on.

---

## Shared Physics, Divided Meaning

Both Echelon and Ashdown experience gravity pulling **toward the plate**.

- No one lives “upside down”
- Buildings rise naturally away from the plate on both sides
- Streets, towers, and infrastructure feel physically identical

The distinction between *above* and *beneath* is **social and political**, not physical.

Language reflects this hierarchy:
- “The elites **above**”
- “Decisions made **up there**”
- “Raids coming **down**”

Over time, these terms became identity rather than orientation.

## Echelon

**Echelon** is the prosperous, governmental face of Starfall Gate.

- Seat of Continuum authority
- Home to high-ranking officials, corporate leadership, and political institutions
- Clean, regulated, architecturally refined
- Heavily surveilled and tightly controlled

Echelon views itself as the crowning achievement of Starfall Gate civilization. Access is restricted, regulated, and status-driven.

Echelon officially denies reliance on Ashdown — while quietly depending on it for survival.

---

## Ashdown

**Ashdown** exists on the opposite face of the same gravity plate and houses the machinery that keeps Echelon alive.

### Purpose and Infrastructure
Ashdown contains:
- Power generation complexes
- Atmospheric processing and purification plants
- Waste reclamation and incineration systems
- Structural maintenance pylons
- Logistics corridors and service tunnels

Byproducts of these systems — ash, particulate waste, heat residue — are vented into Ashdown districts.

Ash settles everywhere.

The name **Ashdown** was not chosen by planners. It emerged naturally and stayed.

---

## Life in Ashdown

Ashdown was never meant to be a city.

It became one because workers had nowhere else to go.

- Maintenance crews
- Power engineers
- Waste processors
- Logistics and infrastructure workers

Most Ashdown residents are **contractually prohibited** from entering Echelon. Entire housing blocks are dedicated to worker families, bolted directly to gravity plate support structures.

Many residents are born, live, and die without ever seeing Echelon firsthand.

Ashdowners often internalize their position as “beneath”:
- Some with resentment
- Some with pride
- Some with resignation

---

## Brightash — The Core of Ashdown

At Ashdown’s center lies **Brightash**.

Brightash is dense, vertical, loud, and alive.

- High-rise towers
- Financial institutions
- Megacorporate offices
- Casinos and entertainment blocks
- Markets, legal and illegal
- Data exchanges and syndicate headquarters

Neon holographic advertisements cut through drifting ash clouds. Transit rails hum overhead. Music, traffic, and crowds never truly stop.

Brightash sells indulgence.

Legitimate businesses operate openly beside black-market fronts — often owned by the same entities. Banks quietly launder credits. Deals made here ripple upward into Echelon whether acknowledged or not.

### Echelon Visitors
Some Echelon residents secretly visit Brightash:
- Disguising their appearance
- Altering speech and mannerisms
- Avoiding facial recognition zones

They indulge in what Echelon forbids, then return above and pretend it never happened.

Ashdown notices.

---

## Access Points Through the Gravity Plate

The gravity plate is extremely resistant to cutting.

- Breaching it is **possible**, but slow and difficult
- Requires specialized tools, dampening equipment, and long-term effort
- Impossible to do quietly without planning

### Official Access Shafts (Echelon-Controlled)
- Heavily guarded
- Armored corridors
- Biometric locks and constant surveillance
- Used for:
    - Infrastructure maintenance
    - Official inspections
    - Security and military raids into Ashdown

Players will see these early but **cannot use them** without explicit authorization.

---

### Secret Access Points (Ashdown-Controlled)

Over decades, smugglers and rogue engineers carved **illegal access shafts** through the plate.

- Narrow maintenance corridors
- Poorly mapped
- Constantly relocated or sealed
- Closely guarded secrets

These often emerge in:
- Basements of Echelon residential towers
- Sealed corporate sublevels
- Forgotten utility rooms
- Structures predating modern zoning records

Echelon officially denies these passages exist.

Ashdown knows better.

---

## Raids from Above

Echelon’s presence in Ashdown is sporadic and violent.

When Ashdown activity threatens stability above:
- Data leaks
- Weapons caches
- Syndicates growing too visible

Armored units descend through official access shafts.

- Streets lock down
- Drones deploy
- Rooms are forcibly cleared

When the raids end:
- Soldiers withdraw
- Nothing is rebuilt
- No explanations are given

Ashdown adapts.

Routes change.  
Markets move.  
The city learns.

---

## Player Progression and Gameplay Rules

### Early Exposure
- Players see guarded access shafts
- NPCs reference “the city on the other side”
- Raids establish Echelon’s power and distance
- Crossing the plate feels impossible

### Unlocking Secret Passage
At a later point, a quest introduces an NPC such as:
- A smuggler
- A fixer
- A former Continuum technician
- A maintenance engineer

This NPC does not “open a door.”

They **teach the player how to use a door that already exists**.

From that point on:
- Certain rooms allow secret transitions between zones
- Movement is discreet
- Guards are bypassed
- Knowledge becomes power

---

## Transition Text (Suggested)

When crossing through a secret access point:

> You step into a narrow service shaft. The hum of machinery deepens around you.
>
> As you descend, your sense of direction wavers — not disorienting, but unmistakable.
>
> For a brief moment, it feels like everything shifts and realigns.
>
> When the hatch opens, the air changes.
>
> You have crossed the gravity plate.

---

## Design Summary

This structure provides:

- A physically consistent sci-fi explanation
- A visible but restricted goal
- Clean zone transitions
- Strong social hierarchy without fantasy elements
- Long-term quest and faction potential

**Echelon governs. Ashdown sustains. Brightash thrives in the shadow between them.**
